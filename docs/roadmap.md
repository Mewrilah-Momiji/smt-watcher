# Roadmap

Goal: a universal tool that analyzes a suspicious email locally in the browser, explains the signs it finds and prepares a report to the right body for the chosen country. Germany comes first.

Name: SMT Watcher (Simple Mail Transfer Watcher), slogan "Spammers' beloved foe". In German: SMT Wächter, "Der Lieblingsfeind der Spammer". The local folder is still called `Applikation`.

Last updated: 2026-11-09. Live: version 0.5.39. Thunderbird: on 2026-11-07 the owner decided not to publish in the catalog (addons.thunderbird.net) any more. The extension is offered as an optional download on the page, from `thunderbird/` on the same server, with `updates.json` for updates. The owner deleted the catalog entry on 2026-11-07. It had no users yet.

Release steps: set `version` in `package.json` and date the changelog heading, run `npm run deploy:senderdb`, compare the live bundles with `dist/` and check that `private/` answers 403, then commit "Release 0.5.x", tag and push. Since 2026-11-05 the Thunderbird extension has its own `thunderbirdVersion`: run `npm run check:thunderbird`, and only if it reports a change raise that version by 0.0.1. Since 2026-11-07 the deploy takes care of the rest: `scripts/thunderbird-release.mjs` runs before the page build, packages the extension into `public/thunderbird/` only if it changed, stops if it changed but the version was not raised or the server already offers that version, and after a successful upload records the fingerprint in `extensions/thunderbird/released.json`, which goes into the release commit. Test the extension in Thunderbird before the deploy, through the gear menu of the add-ons manager with a file from `npm run package:thunderbird`.

Where to resume after the break: try the page with other people's emails from other mail providers, which matters most, as every rule so far was fitted to one recipient's emails. The owner sent the questions to eco, the Fernmeldebüro, SECO and Phishing Initiative on 2026-11-03. The Fernmeldebüro and SECO answered on 2026-11-06, the answers of eco and Phishing Initiative are awaited.

## Principles

- The email itself stays in the browser. Analysis data may fill a sender database, kept free of user-specific details as far as possible. The page must say exactly what leaves it before that starts.
- `connect-src` only allows the abuse contact lookup, which sends the IP address of the sending server after a click, and the page's own server for confirmed reports. Any further exception needs its own decision.
- Reports are never sent automatically. The user reviews and sends them.
- The page never gives the impression that it speaks for a body. The report view says that it is independent and not endorsed by the bodies it names.
- No reports, not even tests, go to real bodies until the tool is finished.
- Assessments use levels with reasons, never a percentage.
- There is no all-clear. The lowest level says "no signs found" and that this does not make an email safe.
- A country only lists bodies from checked sources. Anything else is shown as "not checked".
- Code, comments and Git are in English. The page is in 23 languages, with German and English in `src/i18n.ts` and each other language in its own `src/i18n*.ts` file. All languages but German and English were translated without a native speaker check, so the footer has a link to report translation errors. The texts of reporting bodies only need English and German: other languages fall back to English, and the bodies of a country are also written in its own language.
- Real emails in `SpamMails/` and `LegitimeMails/` are never committed. `LegitimeMails/` holds real mailboxes and is evaluated as counts only.

## Done

- Read `.msg` and `.eml` files, or pasted headers or email source.
- Show the delivery route and evaluate SPF, DKIM and DMARC, including fallbacks for providers that record them elsewhere.
- Content and link analysis without loading anything from the email, including HTML from compressed RTF in `.msg` files.
- Assessment in three levels with reasons and a risk scale. Rules partly derived from reading real spam.
- Reporting for Germany: Bundesnetzagentur, eco Beschwerdestelle and Verbraucherzentrale, with notices on what to expect. See [reporting-channels.md](reporting-channels.md).
- Bundesnetzagentur complaint: phone numbers can also be added by hand, the page says why the body is or is not suggested, and several numbers become one report each.
- Abuse contact: sending IP from the first trusted receiver, an RDAP lookup on click that shows the address and its registry, the full record as a fallback, and an English report without the recipient's address. It names the bodies the user marked as reported. Checked with real answers from all five registries in the web page, and in Thunderbird with an ARIN answer on 2026-11-03.
- Local `.msg` to `.eml` conversion for the Verbraucherzentrale's upload fallback, marked as a reconstruction.
- Thunderbird extension with drafts and no permission to send. Tested in Thunderbird 159 with a test email and an empty profile, without an account and without sending.
- Checking several emails at once in the web page, as a list with the most signs first, up to 200 files, which can be saved as a CSV file created in the browser. Lookups and reports stay per email.
- The brand list is its own file, `src/data/brands.ts`, not in the database, so that the check works offline and in Thunderbird.
- A note for emails with a List-Unsubscribe header and no signs that unsubscribing may be enough, without an all-clear.
- The abuse lookup also reaches national registries behind APNIC (`*.rdap.apnic.net`) and NIC.br, and offers a marked network contact if a record has no abuse role.
- Blocklists are links only, decided by the owner on 2026-11-02: the technical details link to the public check pages of Spamhaus, SpamCop and Barracuda, and the page queries none of them. Spamhaus limits its free service to non-commercial use and refuses large shared hosting, SpamCop calls its list aggressive, and Barracuda's usage pages answer 403 even in a normal browser.
- Export guides for Outlook, Gmail, GMX, WEB.DE and Thunderbird, each with only the steps an official help page confirms. Saving a file from Gmail and both Thunderbird guides were added on 2026-11-03 from Google's and Mozilla's help, read in the owner's Chrome. The guide is grouped by program in folds. Each title reads "program, kind" in every language, which a test checks, and the page splits it.
- An email source saved as `.txt` is read like an `.eml`, for phones, from the first user feedback on 2026-11-03. A byte order mark, plain line breaks and UTF-16 are handled. The guide has a general note for smartphones, without steps for a provider, as GMX names only the desktop for `.eml` and headers, and neither Telekom nor Apple has a help page on it.
- A page "How it works" (`help.html`) on using the tool, the levels, reporting with a warning about abuse contacts, and which data leaves the browser.
- A sober security design with light and dark variants, an interface in 23 languages with a language selection, and reporting as a separate view.
- Advice for senders: a closed section on the result page with six short, general tips for companies, the same for every email. Since 2026-11-05, by the owner's decision, it shows no findings of the email, no ticks, no links to reference pages and no checklist.
- Reporting for every EU country plus Switzerland, the United Kingdom and Norway, with reports in the language of each country. Where no body is checked for a case, the page says so and opens the abuse contact. See [reporting-channels.md](reporting-channels.md).
- With the page in Bulgarian, text mostly in Cyrillic does not count as a foreign script (`forReader` in `src/core/findings.ts`), as the reader writes it too. Other page languages keep the rule unchanged.
- The lists that page and server share (free mail domains, levels, verdicts, authentication results, the domain pattern and the limit of link domains) live in `src/core/sharedLists.ts`. The build writes them to `private/lists.php`, which `private/common.php` includes, and `scripts/deploy.mjs` refuses a `dist/` without it. A test reads the file back in PHP where PHP is installed. Done on 2026-11-05.
- Browser tests of the built page in Chrome, including `.msg` files, the copy button and the download. They do not cover Firefox, the Thunderbird extension, or drags from a real mail program.

## Measurements

Local counts from one recipient and one mail provider, not representative. Spam folder: 193 emails. Legitimate folder: 1872 emails, assumed legitimate. The owner checked 320 of them by hand, all that showed signs and the one with a sender domain from the spam emails, and all 320 are legitimate. The other 1554 showed no signs and were checked only by sender, subject and check results. This moved 2 emails to the spam folder, both missed by the rules. The owner confirmed 5 further doubtful emails as legitimate. Spam with a convincing sender would not show up in such a check.

| Level | Spam | Legitimate |
|---|---|---|
| Many signs | 69% | 0% |
| Some signs | 16% | 1.5% |
| No signs | 15% | 98.5% |

Current counts on 2026-11-03, after the changes below: 234 spam emails (161, 37 and 36) and 1872 legitimate emails (0, 29 and 1843). The fraud warning shows on 121 spam and 4 legitimate emails. Three more emails led to a sign for an unfilled placeholder, to requests for payment or account details counting as login requests, and to a numbered bulk address counting at full strength, which alone moved 31 spam emails to many signs. That one could put a reputable sender with numbered addresses at the top level, which these emails cannot rule out. Eight spam emails picked by the owner led to signs for disguised mathematical letters and a garbled sender name, ELSTER as a brand and public file links without a region, and to a fix for a colon in a sender's name. Each new sign matched no legitimate email, but only one spam email. The provider's own verdict (`X-Spam`, `X-Spamd-Bar`) stays unused: it matched 122 and 80 spam against 21 and 1 legitimate emails, but the spam folder partly comes from that filter, so this confirms the filter with itself. The login rule no longer counts the bare words "Zugangsdaten" and "Anmeldedaten", which caused the warning on 12 legitimate emails, only a request. Exempting signed senders altogether was rejected: 41% of the spam signs its own domain, so a phishing email from a new domain could then show no signs. 0% only says that the rules were fitted to these emails. It does not mean that legitimate emails elsewhere never reach this level.

The brand rule was tightened on 2026-11-02, by the owner's decision on the same emails: more brands, and a domain must be the brand's name unless the brand has many own domains. Measured with the spam folder now at 227 emails and 1876 legitimate emails, this moved spam from 98 to 124 emails at many signs and from 41 to 37 at no signs, and it changed no legitimate level. That is 55%, 29% and 16% for spam, and 1.2%, 3.7% and 95% for legitimate emails. One brand had to keep longer domains, as one legitimate email would otherwise count against it. Brands in the list only help against those brands, and these counts are not a check on new emails.

On 2026-11-03 the owner had the 22 legitimate emails at many signs read in full. 20 were false alarms and now stay below that level, with no spam email losing a level or the fraud warning: newsletters that print a legal notice for their sector (8), a marketplace's padding characters, accepted from a brand's own signed domain (5), carriers named through a shop that signed and links to the carrier (4), HTML attachments from a signed sender (2) and soft hyphens in a subject (1). The other 2, from a supplier with a failed signature and misleading links, were moved to the spam folder at the owner's decision. Counting padding characters from every signed sender as harmless was rejected, as it cost 20 spam emails their level. Spammers could copy the legal notice or a "via" name, and no spam does so yet. The broken umlauts in 62 legitimate `.msg` emails, none in spam, are already in the body Outlook stored, so this page does not cause them.

On 2026-11-03 the owner also had the 57 legitimate emails at some signs read in full. All are genuine, apart from 2 more from the same supplier, which were moved to the spam folder at the owner's decision. An urgency rule was measured and not built: a deadline alone matched 211 legitimate and 18 spam emails, and requiring two of deadline, threat and call to action on an unsigned email matched 6 spam emails, of which only one was not at many signs yet, and 4 legitimate emails. Accepted at no cost on these emails: a link whose text shows the same name on another ending, such as a carrier's .de and .com domains (14 emails), and known shop platforms that sign (11 emails). A spammer's own paypal.example could pass the first of these, which only ever counted as a weak sign. A closing typographic quote was also read as part of a host and raised a punycode finding on a shop email. Exempting a signed sender's public file host or address in the subject costs 2 spam emails their level each, so these stay.

On 2026-11-03 a donation scam sent through a hijacked government account with a valid DKIM signature reached only some signs, as the amount rule missed "500.000 US-Dollar". Allowing "US-Dollar" and "$" found 1 more of the 234 spam emails and none of the 1872 legitimate ones, and put that email at many signs.

An unread part of the spam (59 emails) reached 42%, 39% and 19% before one more round of tuning that saw counts from all emails.

On 2026-11-04 the page was measured on two public collections for the first time, kept outside the repository: 481 phishing emails of 2025 from the Nazario phishing corpus (José Nazario, CC-BY-4.0, <https://monkey.org/~jose/phishing/>) and 974 spam emails of October 2026 from the Untroubled spam archive (Bruce Guenter, free to use, <https://untroubled.org/spam/>). The Untroubled emails carry no authentication results, so they test only the content and link rules. Of the Nazario emails, 192 had no signs, 123 of them passing DKIM and SPF from throwaway or hijacked domains, all in English. Four rules were added for them: the recipient's own domain name in the name of a sender from another domain, a greeting with the recipient's address, more free hosts including IPFS gateways, and English brands. Nazario moved from 140, 149 and 192 emails at many, some and no signs to 239, 131 and 111, Untroubled from 31, 381 and 562 to 43, 373 and 558. The private spam set moved from 161, 37 and 36 to 162, 36 and 36, and 2 of the 1872 private legitimate emails moved to some signs, both through the greeting rule. A webmail lure rule and a callback scam rule were measured and not built, as they matched 9 and 31 legitimate emails.

On 2026-11-04 `baseDomain` moved from 34 known two-part endings to the ICANN part of the Public Suffix List (MPL-2.0), regenerated with `node scripts/update-public-suffixes.mjs`. The private part, such as vercel.app or github.io, stays out, so a free host keeps one base domain. The Nazario corpus had shown ac.in as a sender's base domain. Levels stayed the same on the private test set and on Untroubled, and one Nazario email moved from no signs to some signs. Renew the list before each release, about every six months.

### 4. Sender database

Decided on 2026-11-02: analysis data fills a database, which weakens the promise that nothing leaves the browser.

Stage 1 is built but switched off. `public/api/report.php` takes a report that the user confirmed for one email, after seeing exactly what would be sent. Reports of one campaign are grouped by sender domain and link domains. Domains are stored only as a keyed hash until 5 independent reports. `smtw_group_trust` rates a group from its votes, at least 5 with 80% agreement. A separate "assessment is wrong" report is counted on its own, and it must not change a level by itself, as a spammer can report their own emails as false alarms. The page uses this and may connect to its own server only when built with `--mode senderdb`, which `npm run test:browser:db` tests. It went live on 2026-11-02. The owner decided to keep the privacy text as it is. The server part passed tests against a throwaway MariaDB 10.11 and accepted a test report on the host. `npm run deploy:senderdb` uploads the build with `--mode senderdb` and writes `private/config.php` to the server from `.database.env`. Afterwards, check that `private/` is closed on the host. `npm run deploy` still builds the normal version, and using it after that would remove the feature from the page, while the server would keep accepting reports. `seed_spam` holds starting data from the private spam set, apart from real votes, and it never makes a domain readable. `scripts/seed/` loads it through a temporary script. Since 2026-11-05 public collections may add to it, with the privacy text changed the same day and approved by the owner: `seed-public.json` (outside Git) came from Untroubled September and October 2026 and Nazario 2023 to 2025, 3753 emails. Left out were 272 that give no usable sender (free mail, no readable address), 460 whose sender's domain passed DKIM, 1627 with a DKIM-Signature of the sender's own domain without a verified result, as Untroubled records none, and 1 sender also seen in the Apache lists. That leaves 1393 emails in 958 groups of 914 sender domains, loaded with `node scripts/seed/import-spam.mjs seed-public.json`. Phishing Pot stays out, as its licence (CC BY-NC) forbids commercial use. Stage 2 is built on `public/api/lookup.php`: a button sends the base domain, which is not stored, and the page shows one more finding with a reason. A plain hash would not protect the domain any better, as common domains can simply be tried, and the database uses a keyed one. Many spam reports raise a level to at most "some signs" on their own. Votes for "legitimate" never lower a level, and they are only shown if DKIM confirms the domain. The starting data only leads to a weak hint. Consent is per email for now, which replaces the permanent choice below. Looked at on 2026-11-04: 36 real reports from 3 sources, most likely the owner's own tests, no group with 5 votes and no readable domain. 16 reports carried "assessment is wrong" while the answer agreed with the level, as the mark stayed after changing the answer. Fixed in 0.5.18, and the 16 marks were reset on the server.

- Contributing needs active consent, showing exactly what would be sent. Lookups get their own choice, as they send less. Nothing is sent before the user chooses.
- The choice is stored permanently in the browser, can be changed at any time, and is named in the privacy policy. Local storage is preferred over a cookie, as a cookie would travel to the server with each request.

- Store only what helps to assign spam: the sender's domain, authentication results, finding codes, the level and a coarse date. Check subdomains for personal tokens before storing them.
- Never store the recipient, the local part of addresses, display names, subject, email text, full headers, or the user's IP address. Server logs must not keep it either.
- Count a sender only if it passed authentication, as `From` can be forged. Require several independent reports before an entry counts, and limit reports per source.
- Check emails against our server, and never publish the data, as a readable list would help spammers. The database is reachable only through an endpoint that answers single lookups, with limits per source and no way to list entries. Spammers can still test a single domain of their own through it.
- A lookup tells the server which sender a user received mail from. Send only a shortened hash of the domain, keep no logs of lookups, and name the lookup in the privacy text. The assessment must keep working if the server is unreachable. A match is one more finding with a reason, and no match is no all-clear.
- Measured on the 191 spam emails of one recipient: 128 sender domains, and 21 of them repeat, covering 44% of the emails. Of the later half by date, 26% had a sender domain from the earlier half, and 27% a sending IP. Only 78 emails (41%) have a DKIM pass for their sender domain, covering 70 domains, and only 14 emails come from a repeating one. The provider records no DMARC here, so the rule for counting a sender must rely on DKIM. The sending IP cannot be forged in the same way, so consider it as a further key. Of 1874 emails assumed legitimate, 1 has a sender domain from the spam emails, none has one with a DKIM pass, and none has a sending IP from them. These are counts from one recipient and one provider, so shared mail services can still cause false alarms elsewhere, and they cannot show the benefit of many contributors.
- Needs a server, a removal process, an updated privacy text and the legal check above.

## Ideas, not decided

- Mobile: on an iPad, dragging an email from Apple Mail into the page or into the Files app reportedly creates an `.eml` file. Only third-party sites say so, so test it on a real iPad before naming it in the guide. The page already reads dropped files only and ignores text that comes with them. Fetching emails over IMAP and reading PDFs were suggested and rejected: IMAP needs the user's password and a server, and a PDF has no headers.

- Downloadable threat feeds. Measured on 2026-11-02 on the 227 spam and 1876 legitimate emails, with a snapshot from that day of Phishing.Database, OpenPhish, blocklist.de and Dataplane, matching link hosts and sending IPs. Only Phishing.Database matched: 9 spam emails, 5 already at many signs, 4 at some signs and none of those at no signs, and 27 legitimate emails, 26 of them at no signs. Not worth building as it stands, as it would add false alarms and find no missed spam. The feeds hold current entries while the emails are weeks old, so the sending IP lists, with no match, say little. Repeat only with fresh emails, or with full URLs instead of hosts.
- Check by email: the user forwards an email as an attachment to a service address, gets the findings back, and the email is deleted right after. Needs a server and a separate privacy promise, and it would break the promise that the email stays in the browser. Advised against for now, at most as part of the paid variant.
- Paid variant for IT service providers and company admins, once the free version is public and used. Noted by the owner on 2026-11-02, nothing built. A first concept is kept outside the repository. Checking several emails at once could be an argument for it, but it is free for now.
